# Translation and multilingual editing

These rules apply to every public surface: work pages, recordings, listening clips and player labels, search results, menus, captions, downloadable catalogue descriptions and editorial previews.

## Titles and identities

Retain a work's verified original title in its original language and spelling, including diacritics and Roman script. Follow it with a translation in square brackets where useful. For example: `Die heilige Ente [Le Canard sacré]` and `Die heilige Ente [聖なるあひる]`. The bracketed text is an explanation, never an alternative official title. Apply this consistently wherever the work is mentioned, including track listings and audio credits. Do not infer an original title merely by choosing the existing German field: some works have English or other original titles. Establish the title from the catalogue, score or recording documentation and keep one shared title record.

Keep published album and book titles identifiable as printed; explain them in brackets rather than replacing them. Retain personal names, ensembles, publishers, labels, catalogue numbers, opus numbers, movement numbering, copyright notices and attribution. Japanese explanatory prose may include a conventional Japanese rendering of a name, but must retain the original spelling where needed to identify the artist or release. Never turn Toccata Press into Toccata Classics or confuse similarly named editions.

## Meaning and terminology

Translate complete editorial meaning, not just summaries. Preserve quotations as quotations, attribution, qualifications, dates and historical context. Do not add critical judgments or biographical claims. Use idiomatic French and Japanese with consistent classical-music terminology. Preserve Italian tempo indications and original sung text; provide a bracketed explanation when helpful. Respect the distinction between a work, an arrangement, a movement, an excerpt and a recording.

Consult the approved English and German versions together where useful. Neither automatically overrides a later correction in another language. Record uncertain or conflicting source wording for review rather than silently deciding a new fact.

## Structure and behaviour

Translate navigation, form labels, validation and error messages, accessibility text, search/filter labels and playback controls as well as page prose. Preserve IDs, relationships, filenames, URLs, assets and executable code. Translate visible prose inside legacy interactive content without changing its behaviour. Local links should keep the selected language; external source links remain intact. A language switch should retain the current work, recording, search and fragment.

Keep shared factual data and recording/work relationships in one place. Translations must not create duplicate recordings or disconnected copies of audio clips. Retain preserved archival documents as originals and label their language rather than rewriting historical source files.

## Maintenance and release checks

Comments default to all published languages. An explicit language scope limits the requested wording change; common factual data must remain consistent and any conflict must be surfaced. Include all selected languages in the change review. Never overwrite an intentional single-language explanation during the monthly consistency review.

Before release, check each nonempty translatable field for coverage, compare source and translation for omissions and additions, and verify numbers, names, protected titles, links and HTML structure. Review representative rendered pages and all interactive controls in each language, including Japanese typography and mobile layout. Search by original titles must continue to work. Do not present untranslated English fallback as a completed French or Japanese edition. Report coverage and unresolved exceptions honestly.
